    Constructing and Analyzing a Large-Scale Japanese Counseling Dialogue Dataset with User Feedback
    Zhiyang Qi, Takumasa Kaneko, Keiko Takamizo, Mariko Ukiyo, and Michimasa Inaba.
    Journal of Natural Language Processing, Vol.33, No.4, December 2026, to appear.
    Developing effective psychological counseling AI systems relies heavily on high-quality datasets; however, privacy concerns and data scarcity remain significant barriers. To address these challenges, we present KokoroChat, a large-scale Japanese dataset comprising 6,589 counseling dialogues collected through role-playing by 480 trained counselors, designed to ensure authenticity, professionalism, and safety. Each dialogue is accompanied by comprehensive feedback scores from the client role across 20 dimensions, which we leverage to analyze distinct counselor dialogue strategies. Applying dialogue act annotation and Hidden Markov Models (HMMs), we reveal that high-rated counselors maintain a balance between open questions and reflections during the understanding phase and frequently employ affirmations before offering suggestions, thereby demonstrating superior empathy and support. In contrast, low-rated counselors tend to over-rely on open questions with minimal reflection or affirmation, often becoming trapped in a “question-driven” dialogue pattern. Furthermore, experimental results demonstrate that fine-tuning open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) with KokoroChat exhibits significant potential in both generating high-quality counseling responses and automatically evaluating counseling dialogues.

    Adapting Spoken Dialogue Systems to Low-Resource User Groups through Data Augmentation with a Focus on Dialogue Act Flow and Style
    Zhiyang Qi and Michimasa Inaba.
    Journal of Natural Language Processing, Vol.32, No.4, pp.1030-1061, 2025.
    Spoken dialogue systems (SDSs) often encounter significant challenges when interacting with users who exhibit unique conversational behaviors, such as minors, particularly in low-resource environments with limited data availability. To address these challenges, we present a novel data augmentation framework for enhancing SDS performance, particularly when handling such user groups. The framework employs large language models to extract and model speaker styles and leverages pre-trained language models to simulate diverse dialogue act (DA) histories, ultimately creating a rich and personalized set of training data. By focusing on both the unique speaking styles and distinctive dialogue behavior trajectories of users, the framework improves DA prediction accuracy, thereby guiding the SDS to more effectively adapt to low-resource users with specialized conversational characteristics. Extensive experiments conducted in low-resource settings validate the effectiveness of this approach, demonstrating its potential to improve SDS adaptability and foster the development of more inclusive and responsive systems.

This study focuses on the age of the speaker as a factor that affects the style of communication. We collected a multimodal dialogue corpus with various speaker ages.     CounselTraj: Synthesizing Process-Aware Psychological Counseling Dialogues via Dialogue-Act Trajectory Planning
    Zhiyang Qi and Michimasa Inaba.
    20th Conference of the European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics (EACL 2027), Main Conference (to appear).
    The growing demand for psychological counseling has motivated the development of LLM-based systems that support human counselors, but training such systems requires large-scale, high-quality dialogue data. Authentic counseling transcripts are difficult to collect and share because of privacy concerns, making synthetic dialogues an important alternative. However, existing synthetic datasets, despite covering diverse scenarios and client profiles, often consist of brief exchanges and fail to capture session-level counseling processes. We propose CounselTraj, a dialogue-act trajectory planning framework for expanding brief synthetic dialogues into process-aware, session-level psychological counseling dialogues. CounselTraj learns dialogue-act patterns from role-play counseling dialogues produced by human counselors, plans coordinated trajectories for clients and counselors, and uses them as flexible guidance for long-form dialogue generation. Experiments across three Chinese synthetic counseling datasets show that CounselTraj produces more complete and coherent sessions with higher-quality counselor interventions than both direct expansion and expansion with randomly sampled trajectories. Based on CounselTraj, we construct CounselTrajD, a dataset containing over 10K process-guided Chinese psychological counseling dialogues, and use it to train CounselTrajLM for counseling response generation. Evaluations at both the response and session levels show that CounselTrajLM outperforms publicly available Chinese counseling models in response quality, therapeutic understanding and intervention, and counseling-process coherence and progress. We will publicly release our code and model.

    From Detection to Characterization: A Large-Scale Study of Ragebait on Japanese X
    Zhiyang Qi, Kazuhiro Ito, Jinghui Chen, Hibiki Nakamura, Zhangxuan Chen, Erina Murata, Masaki Chujyo, and Fujio Toriumi.
    25th IEEE/WIC International Conference on Web Intelligence and Intelligent Agent Technology (WI-IAT 2026).
    GitHub Page
    Ragebait refers to online content intentionally designed to provoke anger or outrage and thereby increase attention and engagement. However, reliable large-scale detection and systematic analysis of ragebait remain limited, hindering efforts to understand its prevalence, impact, and mitigation. This study aims to develop an effective ragebait detection framework and to clarify the characteristics of ragebait at scale, providing a basis for understanding and mitigating emotionally provocative content online. We constructed a labeled dataset with the assistance of a large language model (LLM) and trained several Japanese language models for ragebait detection. The resulting ensemble classifier was then applied to a large-scale dataset of Japanese-language posts on X. Our analysis shows that ragebait is more prevalent in politically and socially contentious topics, including politics, discrimination, public health, and interpersonal conflict. Ragebait posts also spread faster and receive more negative reactions than non-ragebait posts, particularly anger, fear, disgust, sadness, and surprise. These findings demonstrate the utility of the proposed detector and provide a large-scale characterization of ragebait in Japanese online discourse.

    When Less Is More: An Empirical Study of Minimal Responses in Counseling Dialogues and the Behavior of LLMs
    Zhiyang Qi.
    2026 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2026), Main Conference.
    Poster
    In psychological counseling, effective support is not always delivered through long, information-rich responses. Minimal responses, such as backchannel cues and concise empathic statements, help convey attentive listening, express empathy, and encourage clients to continue expressing themselves. However, existing counseling dialogue systems and evaluation frameworks often favor explicit, content-rich replies, overlooking the interactional value of brief counselor utterances. This paper presents a systematic cross-lingual analysis of minimal responses across multiple counseling dialogue datasets. We develop a two-stage filtering method based on utterance length and content, followed by contextual verification using a large language model (LLM). Our analysis shows that minimal responses are common in human-collected datasets but substantially underrepresented in LLM-generated ones. We further evaluate current LLMs in manually curated dialogue contexts where human counselors used minimal responses. The results show that strong commercial LLMs are capable of generating minimal responses when explicitly instructed, but still struggle to determine when such responses are appropriate. Counseling-specific models trained on synthetic data perform particularly poorly, tending instead to produce longer and more information-rich responses. Moreover, LLM-based response-quality evaluation may undervalue minimal responses, even when they are interactionally appropriate.

    Bridging the Data Gap: A Hybrid Data Augmentation Approach for Early HIV Screening via Dialogue Classification
    Zhiyang Qi, Haruto Saito, Ryuma Koseki, Mai Suzuki, Sanae Kubota, Toshio Naito, Michimasa Inaba, Chiyoko Kameue, Haruka Matsukura, and Maki Sakamoto.
    3rd International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare (AIiH 2026).
    Poster
    Early detection of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) is critical for improving individual prognosis and preventing transmission, yet diagnosis is often delayed due to subtle symptoms and social stigma. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) suggest the potential for detecting health-related risk signals from everyday conversations. However, developing such systems is hindered by the scarcity of high-quality dialogue data, especially in sensitive medical domains. To address this challenge, we propose a hybrid data augmentation framework for dialogue-based HIV risk screening. The framework constructs synthetic yet realistic dialogue data by leveraging non-fiction resources and integrating narrative-based simulation with dialogue structure preservation. Specifically, we generate data through two complementary strategies: rewriting existing daily conversations while preserving their interactional structure, and generating persona-conditioned dialogues under specific topics. Using the generated data, we fine-tune a language model for HIV risk detection from conversational input. Experimental results show that the proposed method substantially improves screening performance on a high-quality test set, particularly in terms of recall. Furthermore, validation on real-world patient–doctor dialogues demonstrates improved confidence in detecting positive cases, highlighting the practical applicability of the approach.

    Multilingual KokoroChat: A Multi-LLM Ensemble Translation Method for Creating a Multilingual Counseling Dialogue Dataset
    Ryoma Suzuki, Zhiyang Qi, and Michimasa Inaba.
    15th Language Resources and Evaluation Conference (LREC 2026).
    GitHub PagePoster
    To address the critical scarcity of high-quality, publicly available counseling dialogue datasets, we created Multilingual KokoroChat by translating KokoroChat, a large-scale manually authored Japanese counseling corpus, into both English and Chinese. A key challenge in this process is that the optimal model for translation varies by input, making it impossible for any single model to consistently guarantee the highest quality. In a sensitive domain like counseling, where the highest possible translation fidelity is essential, relying on a single LLM is therefore insufficient. To overcome this challenge, we developed and employed a novel multi-LLM ensemble method. Our approach first generates diverse hypotheses from multiple distinct LLMs. A single LLM then produces a high-quality translation based on an analysis of the respective strengths and weaknesses of all presented hypotheses. The quality of “Multilingual KokoroChat” was rigorously validated through human preference studies. These evaluations confirmed that the translations produced by our ensemble method were preferred from any individual state-of-the-art LLM. This strong preference confirms the superior quality of our method’s outputs. The Multilingual KokoroChat is available at https://github.com/UEC-InabaLab/MultilingualKokoroChat.

    Pathophysiological Classification Using Patients' Subjective Pain Expressions
    Zhiyang Qi, Yuji Nozaki, Ryota Abe, Haruka Matsukura, Kazutoshi Fujibayashi, Toshio Naito, and Maki Sakamoto.
    2026 RISP International Workshop on Nonlinear Circuits, Communications and Signal Processing (NCSP'26).
    Slide
    For accurate medical diagnosis, analytical reasoning of diseases is crucial, often utilizing pathophysiological classification based on etiology. Patients’ symptoms are a key element in this process, and onomatopoeia, such as “gan-gan” (pounding) or “kiri-kiri” (stabbing), are frequently used to convey subjective pain. This study aims to support medical diagnosis by analyzing the relationship between onomatopoeia used by patients, their clinical records, and their diagnosed conditions using machine learning techniques. We utilized clinical records from 252 patients at Juntendo University Hospital and performed decision tree analysis. Explanatory variables included phonological features of onomatopoeia, quantified values from adjective pairs representing pain characteristics, age, gender, and clinical test values (CRP, blood pressure). The objective variable was a classification of 11 pathological conditions. The results indicated that quantified values of onomatopoeia and clinical test values were particularly effective for classification. Furthermore, we extracted specific clinical insights, such as a high probability of infectious/inflammatory diseases in younger patients experiencing sharp, long-lasting pain in a narrow area, and a high probability of vascular diseases in middle-aged or older patients with intense, sharp pain.

Crowdsourced data collection methods require strict worker training, and data from real-world counseling environments may raise privacy and ethical concerns.
